Privacy policy

Last updated 26 October 2026

The short version

  • Wombat collects no personal information. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and nothing is sent to me or to anyone else for me.
  • Your documents stay on your Mac. Wombat reads the files and folders you open to show them, and never uploads them.
  • Wombat uses the internet to show an image that a document links to, asking the server that hosts the image directly. The version downloaded from this website also checks once a day for a new version of Wombat; you can turn that off.
  • Wombat's settings and your reading positions stay on your Mac.

Who this covers

This policy covers the Wombat app for Mac, including its Quick Look preview, which is part of the app, and this website. Wombat is made by Sinan Taga (“I” below), who is responsible for this policy. You can reach me at wombat@taga.dev.

What Wombat does not do

  • It has no accounts, sign-in or purchases inside the app.
  • It has no analytics, usage statistics or crash reporting of its own.
  • It contains no advertising or tracking code, mine or anyone else's.
  • It doesn't send me, or anyone else, your documents, file names, settings or anything about how you use it.

Because I receive no data from Wombat, I have nothing to sell, share or lose.

Your documents

Wombat opens the Markdown files and folders you choose: through File › Open, by dropping them on its window or Dock icon, from Finder, or when you press Space on a file for Quick Look. It reads them on your Mac to draw them, and that is all it does with them. It is a viewer, so it never changes your files. If you use Export as PDF or Export as HTML, the new file is written where you choose in the save panel.

Markdown you paste, or copy from a document, is only used for what you asked: pasted text is shown in a window and isn't written to disk by Wombat, and what you copy goes to the clipboard for the app you paste into.

Images from the internet

A Markdown file can point to an image on the web, such as the badges at the top of a README. When a document does, Wombat downloads that image straight from the server the document names, in the app and in Quick Look. That server sees what any server sees when a Mac asks it for a file: your IP address, the address of the image, and basic details of the request such as the app's name and your macOS version. I don't see any of it and I don't run those servers; whoever does has their own privacy policy.

Wombat has no setting to turn this off, so if a document links to images you'd rather not request, open it while your Mac is offline.

Downloaded images are kept in memory while Wombat runs. macOS also keeps its usual cache of web downloads in Wombat's own cache folder on your Mac, so that a repeat request can be quicker, and the network data it keeps for any app, such as which servers accept only secure connections, in a folder of Wombat's own. Both stay on your Mac, I never receive them, and you can delete them any time (see below).

When you click a web link in a document, Wombat hands it to your default browser. Wombat itself doesn't contact the site.

Checking for updates

The version you download from this website keeps itself up to date, using an open-source updater called Sparkle. When Wombat starts, if it hasn't checked in the past day, and then once a day while it runs, it downloads a short list of Wombat's versions (the update feed) from this website. It does the same when you choose Wombat › Check for Updates…. If there's a newer version, Wombat downloads it from GitHub, checks that it's signed by me, and installs it when you quit Wombat.

That request tells GitHub, which hosts this website and the downloads, what any web request does: your IP address, and the app's name and version and the updater's version in the request's User-Agent header, such as “Wombat/0.5.0 Sparkle/2.10.1”, along with the languages your Mac prefers. It sends nothing about you, your documents or how you use Wombat. Sparkle can also send an anonymous profile of your Mac with each check; Wombat has that switched off.

To stop the daily checks, turn off Wombat › Settings… › Check for updates automatically. Check for Updates… still checks when you choose it.

The version from the Mac App Store doesn't check, and contains no updater: the App Store keeps it up to date.

What Wombat keeps on your Mac

To work the way you left it, Wombat remembers a few things in its settings on your Mac:

  • your reading settings: appearance, font, text size, line width, line spacing, zoom, which side the table of contents is on, whether files open in windows or tabs, and what the sidebar shows;
  • the size and arrangement of its windows;
  • your place in the files you've read: the file's location on your Mac and the line at the top of the window, for the last 300 files;
  • for each folder you've opened, the file that was open in it;
  • in the version from this website, whether to check for updates and when it last checked. A new version waits in Wombat's cache folder until it's installed.

macOS itself keeps File › Open Recent and which windows were open, so they can come back when you relaunch. None of this is sent anywhere or synced by Wombat. Your Mac's own backups, such as Time Machine, may include it, as they do for any app's settings.

To remove it, quit Wombat and delete these folders and files, then delete the app. Settings › Restore Defaults only resets the reading settings.

Apple

If you get Wombat from the Mac App Store, Apple handles the download, any payment and its own records under Apple's privacy policy; I don't see your Apple Account or payment details. If you've chosen in System Settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may give me anonymous, summed-up statistics such as downloads and crashes. They can't tell me who you are, and Wombat doesn't collect them itself.

This website

These pages are plain HTML and CSS. They set no cookies, run no scripts, and load no fonts, images or code from anywhere else, and I use no analytics on them. The company that hosts the pages sees the usual details of a web request, such as your IP address, the page you asked for and your browser's name, in order to serve them, under its own privacy policy.

The disk image you can download from this website, and the update feed and new versions that Wombat downloads, are hosted by GitHub, which has its own privacy statement. GitHub shows me how many times each disk image has been downloaded, as a count, with nothing about who downloaded it.

If you write to me

When you email me for help, I receive your email address and whatever you write or attach. I use it to answer you and to fix the problem, I don't share it, and I delete it when it's no longer needed or when you ask. Please don't send documents you wouldn't want me to read; a small made-up example that shows the problem is best.

Your rights

I hold no data about you from the app, so there is nothing to show, correct or delete there. For emails you've sent me, you can ask what I have, ask me to correct it or ask me to delete it at wombat@taga.dev. If you think I've handled your data wrongly, you can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

Wombat isn't aimed at children. Because it collects no data, it collects none from them either.

Changes to this policy

If Wombat ever changes in a way that affects this policy, I'll update this page and its date before the change ships, and say what changed in the release notes.

Contact

Questions about privacy: wombat@taga.dev. For help using Wombat, see the support page.
